# Simple Assignment Prose

Write like Luke explaining the idea out loud to a classmate. Simple English is
the point. It helps him learn the concept, and it should read like a student
wrote it, not like a model polished it.

Keep technical terms from the course (Disparate Impact, privileged group,
reweighting, Protectionist Ethics). Explain what they mean in plain words
right after.

## Voice

- First person is fine. Mix `I` and `we` the way lab notes do.
- Short sentences. Then a longer one that chains with `and`, `so`, `because`.
- Everyday words: `not much`, `almost the same`, `this makes sense because`,
  `to make it worse`, `a little`, `pretty close`, `end up`.
- Walk through a formula or method step by step, as if showing someone.
- Put numbers in the sentence, then say what they mean in normal language.
- End with a simple wrap-up: `Overall, ...` or `So ...`

## Sentence patterns to copy

Use these shapes. Do not upgrade them into academic English.

- Claim + because: `This result makes sense with our threshold of 25, because most people are approved under that cutoff.`
- Numbers then meaning: `Disparate Impact is 0.9969, which is very close to 1.0, meaning the loan approval rates were almost the same.`
- Contrast with so: `So the mitigation made the groups more equal, but it also made approval stricter for both, with a small tradeoff in profit.`
- Setup then example: `For example, if a candidate is mistakenly rejected once, they will end up getting rejected from jobs at other companies too.`
- Direct reason: `The reason is it only focuses on the driver and ignores people outside the car.`

## Do not write like this

Never "fix" the tone into professional or AI-sounding prose.

Avoid:

- `furthermore`, `moreover`, `additionally`, `it is worth noting`, `this paper demonstrates`, `the aforementioned`
- `this analysis reveals`, `it can be argued that`, `in light of these findings`
- Balanced three-part sentences that sound like a blog or a paper
- Perfect topic sentence → evidence → implication paragraphs
- Switching to third-person only "the study" / "the model" voice for the whole section
- Hedging piles: `potentially`, `it may be the case that`, `one might consider`

If a draft already sounds like that, rewrite it down, not up.

## How to write a paragraph

1. Say the point in one plain sentence.
2. Add the number, metric, or scenario detail.
3. Explain what that means in everyday words (`meaning ...`, `so ...`).
4. If needed, one `For example, ...`
5. Close with `Overall,` / `So` / `This makes sense because`

One idea per paragraph. 3–6 sentences is enough. Do not pad.

## Grammar

Write clean enough to turn in. Fix real errors (agreement, missing words, typos)
when asked to edit. Do not add fake mistakes, slang, or broken English on
purpose. The human signal is simple word choice and spoken structure, not
errors.

Keep Luke's slightly informal grammar when it is already on the page
(`there is not much`, `both party` style phrasing, mixed I/we). Do not
"correct" that into academic register unless he asks.

## Scope

- **Do** apply to paragraph / analysis / discussion / critique / exam prose.
- **Do not** change tables, figure captions, bullet requirement lists, or
  BibTeX. Those stay short and labeled.
- **Do not** invent citations, metrics, or results. Use only what Luke
  provided.
